## 摘要

Security researcher Mikko Hyppönen warns that the ransomware criminal "honor code" — where payment guaranteed data would not be published — has collapsed. Groups like ShinyHunters now collect ransom payments and then sell the stolen data regardless, fundamentally changing the risk calculus for ransomware victims.

## 事實內容

- Security expert Mikko Hyppönen warns that the old criminal honor code in ransomware has broken down.
- Previously, victims who paid ransom could be relatively confident their data would not be published; criminals wanted to maintain a reputation for keeping their word.
- Criminals would even disclose how they entered the victim's systems and sometimes promised not to attack again.
- This is no longer the case: English-speaking group ShinyHunters collects ransom payments and then sells the stolen data onward regardless.
- Payment no longer provides any guarantee of data protection or confidentiality for ransomware victims.
- This behavioral shift fundamentally changes the risk equation for organizations considering paying ransoms.
- Organizations should treat all ransomware incidents as full data breaches regardless of whether ransom is paid.

## 受影響系統

- All organizations at risk of ransomware
- Incident response and recovery planning frameworks
- Cyber insurance policy assumptions
