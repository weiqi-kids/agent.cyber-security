## 摘要

Portugal's CNCS published the Risks & Conflicts 2021 Report covering 2020 trends: significant increases in cybersecurity incidents and cybercrime driven by COVID-19, remote work, and digital acceleration; top threats include phishing, malware, ransomware, and disinformation.

## 事實內容

The Risks & Conflicts 2021 Report by CNCS's Cybersecurity Observatory focuses on 2020 data while projecting 2021 and 2022 trends. Key findings:

**Notable highlights**:
- "There was a significant increase in the volume of cybersecurity incidents and cybercrime numbers in 2020"
- "The most relevant cyberthreats in 2020 were: phishing/smishing, malware-infected systems, ransomware, various forms of intrusion, different types of fraud/scams, sextortion, and digital disinformation"
- "There was an increase in the perception of risk of suffering a cybersecurity incident in the national cyberspace of interest in 2020 and 2021"
- "There is a perception that the national cyberspace of interest is more or equally capable in 2021 compared to 2020"

**Context**: The COVID-19 pandemic marked this period, with coincidence observed between increased incidents/reports and the emergence of social confinement, remote work, and intense digital usage.

**Methodology**: Analysis combines CNCS-produced indicators, contributions from various partners, community survey data, and public data from other entities.

- Remote workers and digital service users
