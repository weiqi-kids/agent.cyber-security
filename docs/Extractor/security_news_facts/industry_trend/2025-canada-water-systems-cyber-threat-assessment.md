## 摘要

Canadian Cyber Centre assesses cyber threats to water infrastructure, identifying ransomware as most significant threat to reliable water supply.

## 事實內容

The Canadian Centre for Cyber Security released a comprehensive threat assessment addressing cyber risks to Canada's water systems, identifying critical vulnerabilities and mitigation strategies.

**Key Threats:**

**Primary Adversaries (Ranked by Likelihood):**

1. **Financially Motivated Cybercriminals** (Most Likely Threat):
   "Ransomware is almost certainly the most significant cyber threat to the reliable supply of water in Canada due to the potential impacts against OT systems."

2. **State-Sponsored Actors**:
   Nation-state adversaries with strategic objectives, potentially pre-positioning for disruption during geopolitical tensions.

3. **Non-State Hacktivists**:
   Ideologically motivated groups targeting infrastructure for political or social causes.

**Vulnerabilities:**

**Operational Technology (OT) Risks:**
OT networks controlling physical water treatment and distribution processes face particular risk. Many systems:
- Were designed before cybersecurity considerations
- Lack built-in security features
- Connect to internet without proper segmentation
- Run on outdated, unsupported software

**Small Utility Challenges:**
Small water utilities often lack:
- Dedicated cybersecurity expertise
- Budget for security investments
- Resources for 24/7 monitoring
- Ability to replace aging infrastructure

**Common Weaknesses:**
- Internet-exposed systems with default passwords
- Lack of network segmentation between IT and OT
- Insufficient access controls and authentication
- Limited incident response capabilities

**Mitigation Strategies:**

**Authentication and Access Control:**
- Implement phishing-resistant multi-factor authentication (MFA)
- Change default passwords immediately
- Enforce strong password policies
- Implement role-based access control

**Network Architecture:**
- Establish network segmentation between IT and OT systems
- Remove unnecessary internet connectivity from OT networks
- Implement firewalls and demilitarized zones (DMZs)
- Monitor network traffic for anomalies

**Security Operations:**
- Conduct regular security assessments and penetration testing
- Develop and test incident response plans
- Maintain system backups with regular testing
- Patch vulnerabilities promptly

**Supply Chain Security:**
- Vet vendors and service providers
- Secure remote access for maintenance
- Review third-party access permissions regularly

**Strategic Importance:**

The assessment emphasizes that water system cybersecurity represents both a **public safety priority** (ensuring safe drinking water and wastewater treatment) and a **national security priority** (protecting critical infrastructure from adversarial disruption).

**Implication:**
Small and medium water utilities face disproportionate risk due to resource constraints, requiring government support, information sharing, and possibly regulatory frameworks to achieve adequate security posture.

## 受影響系統

- Water treatment facilities
- Water distribution systems
- Wastewater treatment plants
- Operational technology (OT) controlling physical processes
- Small and medium water utilities across Canada
