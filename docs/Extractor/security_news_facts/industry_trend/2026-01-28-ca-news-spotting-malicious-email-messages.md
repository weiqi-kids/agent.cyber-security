## 摘要

Canada's Cyber Centre updates awareness guidance on identifying malicious emails, covering phishing, spear-phishing, and business email compromise indicators.

## 事實內容

The Canadian Centre for Cyber Security published updated awareness guidance ITSAP.00.100 on spotting malicious email messages. Email remains one of the most common initial access vectors for cyber attacks.

Key indicators of malicious emails covered:
- **Sender address spoofing**: Emails appearing to come from legitimate domains but using slight variations or lookalike domains
- **Urgency and emotional manipulation**: Messages creating false urgency (e.g., "act now," "account suspended") to pressure recipients into hasty action
- **Suspicious attachments**: Unexpected files especially .exe, .zip, .docm, or password-protected archives
- **Malicious links**: URLs that redirect to credential harvesting pages, often disguised with URL shorteners or typosquat domains
- **Spear-phishing indicators**: Highly personalized messages referencing real colleagues, projects, or events to build credibility
- **Business Email Compromise (BEC)**: Impersonation of executives or finance staff to authorize fraudulent wire transfers
- **AI-generated phishing**: Increasingly convincing phishing content generated using large language models

Practical advice includes: hover over links before clicking, verify sender domains carefully, use email security filters, and report suspicious messages to IT/security teams. Organizations are encouraged to run phishing simulation exercises.

## 受影響系統

- Email systems (all platforms)
- Organizations across all sectors
- Individual users
