## 摘要

Alleged alliance "Scattered LapSus Hunters" comprising three hacker groups (Scattered Spider, LapSus, ShinyHunters) emerged, potentially integrating different attack methods and resources.

## 事實內容

Recent social media platforms and threat intelligence indicate an alliance called "Scattered LapSus Hunters" comprising multiple hacker organizations is attracting attention. Although the alliance has not yet provided concrete technical evidence proving successful intrusion into an international cloud and network service provider's database, and the enterprise found no new vulnerabilities or intrusion indicators, its influence remains significant.

In August this year, the enterprise confirmed its third-party partner was attacked by another hacker organization "ShinyHunters," causing partial data leakage. Notably, the security incident occurred in third-party systems, not the enterprise's core infrastructure, highlighting the importance of third-party supply chain security management.

**Alliance Composition (Suspected):**
According to available intelligence analysis, "Scattered LapSus Hunters" allegedly comprises three known hacker organizations:

1. **Scattered Spider**: Known for complex social engineering techniques
2. **LapSus**: Targets large tech companies with attacks
3. **ShinyHunters**: Long-active on dark web conducting large-scale data leaks

If this alliance truly exists, it represents integration of different attack methods and resources, potentially escalating cyber threats and bringing greater challenges to enterprise security protection.

**Attack Methods of Three Organizations:**

**Scattered Spider:**
- Known for advanced social engineering
- Targets financial, retail, aviation, and supply chain critical industries
- Commonly impersonates IT support personnel
- Uses vishing, SIM-swapping, MFA bombing to bypass multi-factor authentication
- Steals internal credentials for lateral movement
- Law enforcement and security agencies issued joint advisories and prevention recommendations

**LapSus (Microsoft codename DEV-0537):**
- Known for targeting large tech or supplier companies using internal credentials and remote desktop tools to obtain sensitive data
- Threatens or directly uploads to dark web for extortion
- Methods appear to "not necessarily rely on advanced zero-days" but use continuous social engineering, insider assistance, or stolen management credentials
- Distributes stolen data on public platforms (e.g., Telegram) to apply pressure

**ShinyHunters:**
- Since 2020, publicly disclosed multiple large-scale data leaks
- Victim scope includes e-commerce, cloud services, and application user data
- Uses social engineering and exploits unpatched system vulnerabilities
- Quickly organizes stolen data, immediately sells on dark web or publicly leaks
- Creates continuous and high-impact data leakage incidents

**Common Pattern:**
All three observed organizations commonly use social engineering as primary intrusion method, typically including phishing emails, SIM swapping, and internal account recruitment.

**Recommended Mitigations:**
1. Organizations must not only strengthen existing technical protection measures but also enhance employee security awareness and strengthen prevention of social engineering attack risks
2. Comprehensively review supply chain and third-party partner security measures to avoid becoming hacker attack entry points
3. Establish cross-department collaborative security response plans ensuring rapid detection, notification, and response during large-scale data leaks or ransomware attacks
4. Enhance organizational resilience and defense capabilities when facing attacks from multiple hacker organizations

## 受影響系統

- Enterprise cloud infrastructure and third-party services
- Supply chain partners and vendors
- Financial, retail, aviation, and technology sectors
