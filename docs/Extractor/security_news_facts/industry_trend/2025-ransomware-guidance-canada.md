## 事實內容

The Canadian Centre for Cyber Security published comprehensive ransomware guidance identifying it as "the most prevalent cyber threat facing Canadians."

**Threat Overview:**
During attacks, criminals deploy malicious software to encrypt, steal, or delete data while demanding payment for restoration. Impacts include:
- Business downtime
- Permanent data loss
- Intellectual property theft
- Privacy breaches
- Reputational harm
- Recovery expenses

**Conti Group Case Study (Four-Stage Attack):**

**Stage 1 - Reconnaissance:**
Actors "gather information to identify high-value targets such as hospitals" and organizations holding sensitive data, leveraging internet searches, system scans, and dark web intelligence.

**Stage 2 - Intrusion:**
Criminals gain access through stolen credentials or spear-phishing emails containing malicious attachments disguised as Word, Excel, or PDF files that deploy malware like TrickBot or IcedID.

**Stage 3 - Infection:**
Once initial compromise occurs, attackers install command-and-control software, exploit unpatched vulnerabilities, and "move laterally across the victim network" using tools like PSExec to spread infection network-wide.

**Stage 4 - Impact:**
Ransomware deploys while attackers employ "double extortion"—demanding payment while threatening public data disclosure.

**Conti Gang Statistics:**
- Over 450 confirmed Conti attacks since January 2020
- At least 24 Canadian victims claimed in 2021
- Average ransom demand: $373,902 USD (September 2021)
- Machinery, professional services, real estate, and retail sectors most targeted

**Critical Finding:**
The Centre emphasizes that "basic cyber security practices would prevent the vast majority of ransomware incidents in Canada."

**Organizational Guidance:**
- Ransomware Playbooks and recovery protocols
- Multi-factor authentication implementation
- Regular security updates and patch management
- Employee cybersecurity training
- Incident response and IT recovery planning
- Backup and data protection strategies

**Guidance for All Canadians:**
- Password and passphrase best practices
- Phishing recognition and avoidance
- Device and network security fundamentals

**Incident Reporting:**
Victims should report incidents to enable protective guidance and services from Cyber Centre.

The guidance emphasizes that ransomware remains a persistent and financially motivated threat, but that fundamental security practices significantly reduce organizational risk exposure.

## 受影響系統

- All organizational systems and networks
- Hospitals and healthcare systems (high-value targets)
- Machinery, professional services, real estate, retail sectors
- Systems running Word, Excel, PDF applications (phishing vectors)
- Networks vulnerable to lateral movement (PSExec)
- Backup and data protection systems
