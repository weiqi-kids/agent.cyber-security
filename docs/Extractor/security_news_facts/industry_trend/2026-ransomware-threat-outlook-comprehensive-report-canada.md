## 摘要

Canadian Cyber Centre releases comprehensive ransomware threat outlook identifying 26% annual increase, top threat actors (Akira, Play, Medusa), and emerging AI-powered attack tactics.

## 事實內容

The Canadian Centre for Cyber Security published its comprehensive Ransomware Threat Outlook 2025-2027, concluding that ransomware poses a persistent and evolving danger to all Canadian organizations.

**Threat Assessment:**
Ransomware incidents showed "a 26% average year-over-year increase from 2021 to 2024" with trajectory expected to continue through 2027. All organizations—regardless of size or sector—face vulnerability.

**Key Predictions (2025-2027):**
- Threat actors will "almost certainly" remain opportunistic and financially motivated
- Ransomware operators will "very likely" continue adopting AI and cryptocurrency advancements
- Exfiltration-only attacks (without encryption) represent emerging tactical shift
- Multi-extortion methods expanding beyond encryption to include DDoS and supply chain targeting

**Top Three Threats to Canada (2024):**

1. **Akira**: Emerged April 2023, likely connected to disbanded Conti group; uses double extortion; targets manufacturing and telecommunications
2. **Play**: Emerged June 2022; shifted to ransomware-as-a-service model November 2023; focuses on IT and professional services
3. **Medusa**: Operating since June 2021; targets critical infrastructure and communications sectors

Core membership predominantly Russian-speaking, operating from Commonwealth of Independent States territories, though affiliates operate globally.

**Attack Vectors:**
- Unpatched software vulnerabilities
- Compromised credentials and phishing
- Remote Desktop Protocol exploitation
- Initial access brokers selling network penetration
- Managed service provider (MSP) compromise (particularly impacting SMBs)

**Industry Sectors at Risk:**
- Public sector (government entities)
- Private logistics and supply chain
- Retail and health services
- Education technology
- Energy sector
- Critical infrastructure (hospitals, utilities)

**Emerging Trends:**

**AI Integration:**
Cybercriminals leverage generative AI and large language models for:
- Malware development
- Deepfake creation
- Victim negotiation automation
- Social engineering

**Cryptocurrency Evolution:**
Threat actors exploit:
- Privacy coins (Monero, Zcash)
- Obfuscation techniques (chain hopping, mixers)
- Financial Transactions and Reports Analysis Centre identified ransomware payment laundering as "the most prevalent form" of virtual currency money laundering in 2023

**Exfiltration-Only Attacks:**
Hunters International rebranded to World Leaks in January 2025, distributing custom data extraction tools. This approach proves faster than encryption-based ransomware.

**Statistics and Impact Data:**
- 13% of Canadian businesses reporting cyber incidents identified ransomware as attack method (2% increase since 2021)
- Recovery costs from all cyber incidents "doubled to CAD 1.2 billion" in 2023 (from CAD 200 million in 2019-2021)
- Only 22% of Canadian businesses provided formal cyber training to non-IT employees
- 11% decrease in organizations employing dedicated cyber security workers, partially offset by MSP usage
- Fiscal 2024-2025: Cyber Centre issued 336 pre-ransomware notifications, preventing up to "CAD 18 million" in economic losses

**Notable Case Studies (5 Real-World Canadian Incidents):**

1. **Public Sector #1**: Multi-month dormant breach; compromised staff, customer, applicant personal/medical/financial data; extended service outages during rebuild
2. **Public Sector #2**: Contained within days; recovery costs in millions; some systems unusable for months
3. **Logistics Companies**: Breached customer personal information; cases referred to Office of Privacy Commissioner
4. **Health Retailer**: Multi-day operational shutdown; compromised HR/finance and employee data
5. **Education Technology**: Compromised credential exploitation affecting millions; ransom paid despite attacker's assurance of data deletion; subsequent re-extortion attempts documented

**Mitigation Strategies:**

**Essential Cyber Hygiene:**
- Regular software updates and automatic patching
- Multi-factor authentication implementation
- Routine data backups (stored separately)
- Employee security awareness training
- Incident response planning

**Organizational Measures:**
- Critical evaluation of cyber insurance policies (ensure attackers cannot access coverage details)
- MSP security assessment and vetting
- Network segmentation and monitoring
- Avoid ransom payment (no guarantee of data recovery; threat of re-extortion)

**Reporting Protocol:**
Organizations should report attacks to local authorities, Canadian Anti-Fraud Centre, and Cyber Centre via My Cyber Portal or contact@cyber.gc.ca.

**Debunked Misconceptions:**
- "We're too small to be targeted": SMBs face heightened vulnerability through supply chain integration and MSP dependencies
- "Cyber tools are unnecessary": Basic hygiene significantly reduces entry-point exploitability
- "Paying ransoms recovers data": No guarantee of unlock or data deletion; re-extortion is common
- Personal data exposure is harmless: Compromised information remains in dark web ecosystems indefinitely

**Geopolitical Dimensions:**
Some governments provide direct resources to cybercriminals, while others tacitly permit attacks targeting adversaries. Examples:
- Conti's 2022 threats against Western nations during Ukraine invasion
- Iranian-linked groups offering payment incentives for attacks on specific geopolitical targets

## 受影響系統

- All Canadian organizations (universal threat)
- Manufacturing and telecommunications (Akira targets)
- IT and professional services (Play targets)
- Critical infrastructure and communications (Medusa targets)
- Managed service providers (MSPs)
- Small and medium businesses (SMBs)
- Public sector, logistics, retail, health services, education technology, energy sector
