## 摘要

Canada's Cyber Centre publishes threat assessment on cyber risks to marine transportation, highlighting growing digitalization vulnerabilities in port operations, vessel navigation, and supply chain logistics.

## 事實內容

The Canadian Centre for Cyber Security published a threat assessment report on the cyber threats facing the marine transportation sector. As digitalization accelerates across shipping, ports, and maritime logistics, the attack surface has expanded significantly.

Key findings:
- **Digitalization risks**: Modern vessels increasingly rely on networked systems including GPS/GNSS navigation, Automatic Identification Systems (AIS), Electronic Chart Display and Information Systems (ECDIS), and cargo management platforms — all of which are potential attack vectors
- **GPS/GNSS spoofing and jamming**: State-sponsored and criminal actors have demonstrated capability to disrupt or manipulate maritime navigation signals, with implications for vessel safety and cargo security
- **Port operations**: Automated port systems, terminal operating systems, and logistics software are targeted for disruption and data theft
- **Supply chain implications**: Marine transportation is critical to global trade; disruptions can cascade into broader supply chain and economic impacts
- **Threat actors**: Both state-sponsored groups (particularly from Russia, China, and Iran) and criminal ransomware operators target marine sector
- **Key incidents referenced**: Attacks on major ports and shipping companies have demonstrated potential for operational disruption

Recommendations include implementing maritime-specific cybersecurity frameworks, segmenting operational technology (OT) networks, and enhancing information sharing among port operators and shipping companies.

## 受影響系統

- Vessel navigation systems (GPS/GNSS, AIS, ECDIS)
- Port terminal operating systems
- Cargo management and logistics platforms
- Maritime communication networks
- Supply chain and freight management systems
