## 摘要

Canadian Cyber Centre releases guidance on content provenance using cryptographic methods to combat AI-generated misinformation and establish verifiable digital content records.

## 事實內容

The Canadian Centre for Cyber Security published ITSP.10.005 on public content provenance for organizations.

**Purpose:**
Organizations need to establish verifiable records of their digital content to combat misinformation and maintain audience trust. The guidance addresses how "information on the Internet cannot be relied on consistently as a source of truth," particularly given AI-generated content and malicious activities.

**Core Framework:**
The approach treats provenance like a notary service—a trusted third party validates content details (hash, timestamp, origin) using cryptographic methods rather than paper documents. Organizations document the content's history without asserting its truthfulness, leaving verification decisions to audiences.

**Implementation Steps:**

1. **Assess needs**: Determine whether provenance addresses specific trust challenges

2. **Select appropriate scope**: Choose which content requires provenance (critical items vs. all content)

3. **Choose technologies**: Match tools to organizational objectives and resource availability

4. **Plan lifecycle**: Consider duration requirements and verification accessibility

**Trust Mechanisms (Key Technologies):**

**Trusted timestamps (RFC 3161 standard):**
Cryptographic time-stamping performed by third parties

**Cryptographic identities:**
PKI-based binding of individuals/devices to content

**Blockchain/digital ledgers:**
Tamper-proof, decentralized records

**Coalition for Content Provenance and Authenticity (C2PA):**
Industry standard using manifests and content credentials

**Primary Benefits:**

**Security:**
Verifies legitimate sources, prevents unauthorized alterations

**Safety:**
Supports refutation of inaccurate claims about organizations

**Transparency:**
Establishes verifiable metadata about content history

**Auditability:**
Creates reviewable records of content and modifications

**Fairness:**
Documents ownership, rights, and attribution clearly

**Context:**
This guidance addresses the growing challenge of distinguishing authentic organizational content from AI-generated misinformation and malicious impersonation in the digital ecosystem.

The framework provides organizations with cryptographic tools to establish provenance without requiring them to assert content truthfulness, empowering audiences to make informed trust decisions.

## 受影響系統

- Organizational digital content publishing systems
- Content management systems (CMS)
- Cryptographic timestamping infrastructure (RFC 3161)
- Public Key Infrastructure (PKI) systems
- Blockchain/digital ledger systems
- C2PA-compliant content authentication platforms
