## 摘要

CERT.at provides critical feedback on EU Commission's cybersecurity blueprint revision, highlighting scope gaps and coordination issues.

## 事實內容

In March 2025, CERT.at published critical feedback on the European Commission's draft update to the 2017 cybersecurity blueprint. The analysis identifies several structural weaknesses in the proposed framework.

**Scope Issues:**
The draft overemphasizes DNS as a critical dependency while neglecting other critical infrastructure vulnerabilities including routing systems, Content Delivery Networks (CDNs), and cloud services. This narrow focus risks missing significant attack surfaces and points of failure.

**Incident Trigger Definitions:**
The document narrowly focuses on malicious cyberattacks, overlooking non-malicious causes of large-scale outages such as software bugs, human error, and supply chain disruptions. CERT.at references the CrowdStrike incident affecting 8.5 million systems as an example of how non-malicious failures can create massive disruptions.

**Geopolitical Risk Considerations:**
Supply chain vulnerabilities extend beyond security breaches to include vendor sanctions, export restrictions, and production disruptions amid geopolitical tensions. The blueprint should address how dependencies on specific vendors or regions create strategic vulnerabilities.

**Coordination Gaps:**
The blueprint lacks clarity on crisis management chains of command between national and EU authorities. CERT.at emphasizes that communication infrastructure must bridge public-private sectors rather than creating isolated EU-only systems.

**Recommendations:**
The feedback emphasizes the need to:
- Broaden threat perspectives beyond purely malicious attacks
- Strengthen institutional coordination frameworks
- Address geopolitical supply chain risks
- Clarify crisis management command structures

This represents CERT.at's contribution to shaping EU-level cybersecurity strategy and operational frameworks.

## 受影響系統

- EU cybersecurity policy framework
- Critical infrastructure protection across EU
- National and EU-level cybersecurity authorities
- Public-private sector coordination mechanisms
