## 摘要

CERT.at shares lessons from the CSIRTs Network applicable to the EU's proposed European Network of SOCs under the Cyber Solidarity Act, warning that technical tools alone are insufficient and governance/trust structures are the harder problem.

## 事實內容

Originally written in January 2021 at the request of the European Commission's DG-CNECT, and updated in July 2023 to reflect the Cyber Solidarity Act (CSoA) context. Presented at the CSIRTs Network Meeting in Stockholm in June 2023.

**Background:**
- The NIS Directive (2016) established the CSIRTs Network (CNW).
- The EU Cybersecurity Strategy (2020) proposed a similar network for Security Operation Centres (SOCs).
- The 2023 Cyber Solidarity Act codifies national SOCs and cross-border SOC platforms into EU regulation.

**Key lessons from the CSIRTs Network applied to a potential SOC Network:**
1. **Technical tools are the easy part**: The same toolstack developed for the CSIRTs Network can almost directly support SOC networks (e.g., MISP, IntelMQ). Tools are not the bottleneck.
2. **Trust and governance are the hard part**: Effective collaboration requires long-standing trust relationships, shared operational culture, and clear governance frameworks. These take years to build.
3. **Mandate matters**: CSIRTs in the CNW have clear, legally-defined mandates. SOCs vary widely in their authority, scope, and sector focus, making standardisation difficult.
4. **Funding structure**: EU funding for multiple consortia is a sound approach; point-to-point bilateral arrangements do not scale.
5. **Coordination vs. command**: A network of peers works better than a hierarchical structure; the CNW succeeded partly because it avoided top-down command relationships.

CERT.at also noted that the CSoA approach has structural flaws that a separate position paper was planned to address.
