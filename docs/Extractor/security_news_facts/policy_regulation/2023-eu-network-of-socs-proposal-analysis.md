## 摘要

CERT.at analyzes EU proposal for coordinated Security Operation Centers network, emphasizing organizational over technical challenges.

## 事實內容

The European Union proposed coordinating Security Operation Centers (SOCs) across member states, similar to the existing CSIRTs Network. CERT.at's analysis emphasizes that successful collaboration requires far more than technical infrastructure.

**Key Proposals:**

**Trust Building:**
Establishing trust through repeated face-to-face interactions and physical meetings, not just virtual collaboration.

**Optimal Group Sizing:**
Maintaining groups of 15-20 members to sustain openness and meaningful participation. Larger groups risk becoming performative rather than collaborative.

**Shared Context:**
Ensuring participants face similar challenges and aren't direct competitors. Alignment of interests is critical for genuine information sharing.

**Resource Allocation:**
Dedicated resources and secretariat support are essential. Voluntary participation without institutional commitment fails.

**Low-Friction Sharing:**
Implementing mechanisms that make information sharing easier than withholding it.

**Major Concerns:**

**Beyond Technical Solutions:**
The author warns: "one cannot just focus on the technical aspects" of collaboration. Shared MISP platforms and technical infrastructure are necessary but insufficient.

**Critical Obstacles:**
- **Vendor-Customer Conflicts**: Commercial SOC providers competing while expected to collaborate
- **Competitive Tensions**: Organizations reluctant to share intelligence that provides competitive advantage
- **Data Privacy**: Legal and regulatory barriers to cross-border information sharing
- **Tragedy of the Commons**: Freeloaders exploiting shared resources without contributing equally

**Success Requirements:**
- Alignment of commercial interests beyond regulatory compliance
- Genuine motivation to collaborate rather than checkbox compliance
- Clear value proposition for all participants
- Governance preventing free-riding behavior

**CERT.at's Position:**
Effective SOC networks require organizational design, incentive alignment, and trust-building that cannot be mandated through regulation alone. The proposal must address human and institutional factors, not just technical capabilities.

This analysis draws from CERT.at's experience with the successful CSIRTs Network to inform SOC network design.

## 受影響系統
