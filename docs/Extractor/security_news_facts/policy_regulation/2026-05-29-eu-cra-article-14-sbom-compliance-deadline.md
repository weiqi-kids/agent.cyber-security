## 摘要

EU Cyber Resilience Act (CRA) Article 14 vulnerability reporting obligation becomes mandatory on September 11, 2026, requiring manufacturers of connected products to issue early warnings within 24 hours of discovering actively exploited vulnerabilities.

## 事實內容

The EU Cyber Resilience Act (CRA) enforcement timeline is entering its critical phase. **Article 14 — Vulnerability Reporting Obligation** will become mandatorily enforceable on **September 11, 2026**.

**Key Requirements:**
- All manufacturers of digital-element products sold in the EU market must issue an early warning within **24 hours** of learning about an "Actively Exploited Vulnerability"
- This obligation applies globally — not only to EU-based companies but to any manufacturer seeking EU market access
- The regulation creates significant impact on SBOM (Software Bill of Materials) management for IoT and connected device manufacturers

**Scope:** The CRA's most impactful provisions extend to all connected manufacturers worldwide, establishing a new international standard for vulnerability disclosure timelines. Non-compliance risks include market exclusion from the EU.

**Key Challenge — SBOM Management:** The regulation significantly raises the bar for supply chain transparency. Manufacturers must maintain up-to-date SBOMs to identify affected components quickly enough to meet the 24-hour reporting window, representing a major operational shift for the global IoT industry.

**Timeline Context:** This September 11, 2026 deadline represents the first of several CRA enforcement milestones, with additional provisions coming into force in subsequent years.

## 受影響系統

- All connected digital-element products (IoT devices, smart appliances, industrial control systems, etc.) sold in the EU market
- Global manufacturers and distributors targeting the EU market
- Software vendors whose components are incorporated into connected products
