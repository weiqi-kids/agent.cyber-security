## 摘要

Iran has formally institutionalized a two-tiered internet system through regulation passed in July 2025. Citizens no longer have default access to the global internet; access is a privilege granted based on loyalty and professional necessity. Special "white SIM cards" bypass state filters for government officials and approved journalists.

## 事實內容

- In July 2025, Iran's government council passed a regulation formally institutionalizing a two-tiered internet hierarchy.
- Under this system, access to the global internet is no longer a default for citizens but a privilege granted based on loyalty and professional necessity.
- "White SIM cards" — special mobile lines issued to government officials, security forces, and approved journalists — bypass the state's filtering apparatus entirely.
- Ordinary citizens are subject to increasingly restrictive filtering and surveillance of internet activity.
- The two-tiered system creates a stark information asymmetry between the regime and the general population.
- Security researchers and policy experts warn this model could be replicated by other authoritarian governments.
- The structure enables the regime to maintain operational security for its cyber activities while controlling civilian information access.

- Global internet freedom standards and norms
