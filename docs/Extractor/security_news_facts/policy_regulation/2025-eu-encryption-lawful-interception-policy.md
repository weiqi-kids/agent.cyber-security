## 摘要

European Commission unveils roadmap for law enforcement access to encrypted data; CERT.at raises concerns about fundamental encryption security contradictions.

## 事實內容

In June-July 2025, the European Commission unveiled a comprehensive roadmap addressing law enforcement access to digital data, covering data retention, lawful interception, digital forensics, decryption, standardization, and AI solutions.

**Key Proposal:**
A "Technology Roadmap on encryption to identify and evaluate solutions that enable lawful access to encrypted data" is planned for development by 2026.

**CERT.at's Primary Concern:**
The cybersecurity community views this as attempting "the development of a square that is also round"—balancing encryption strength with backdoor access fundamentally undermines security principles. Strong encryption and lawful access backdoors are mathematically incompatible without compromising security for all users.

**Expert Group Formation:**
The Commission is recruiting an expert group with diverse backgrounds in cybersecurity, home affairs, telecommunications, and fundamental rights to navigate these technical and policy trade-offs.

**CERT.at's Position:**
While acknowledging law enforcement frustrations with encrypted communications, CERT.at argues policymakers should prioritize addressing social media's algorithmic effects on society rather than pursuing surveillance capabilities. The analysis suggests the real democratic threat lies in algorithmic manipulation, not encrypted private communications.

**Policy Context:**
This roadmap represents ongoing EU efforts to balance privacy, security, and law enforcement needs in the digital age, continuing debates over encryption policy that have spanned multiple years.
