## 摘要

CERT.at reviews NIS 2.0 Recitals, criticizing uniform EU thresholds and broad DNS provider coverage while welcoming financial sector inclusion.

## 事實內容

Otmar Lendl's January 2021 analysis of NIS 2.0 Recitals (non-binding explanatory text) identifies both concerns and positive developments.

**Key Concerns:**

**Uniform EU-Wide Thresholds:**
The proposal applies uniform entity size thresholds across all EU member states. This approach is potentially problematic for smaller member states where critical infrastructure operates at smaller scales than in larger economies. One-size-fits-all thresholds may miss critical entities in small nations while over-capturing in larger ones.

**Broad DNS Provider Coverage:**
Lendl strongly opposes expansive DNS provider coverage, arguing it would capture personal devices running recursive nameservers. This undermines the Internet's fundamentally decentralized design and imposes regulatory burdens on individual users running standard network infrastructure.

The proposal risks conflating professional DNS service provision with incidental DNS functionality on personal or small business networks.

**Positive Developments:**

**Financial Sector Inclusion:**
The proposal brings financial sectors into the cybersecurity framework, addressing previous gaps in critical infrastructure coverage.

**Vulnerability Disclosure Procedures:**
Requiring entities to establish vulnerability disclosure processes represents progress in coordinated security practices.

**International Cooperation:**
Provisions for international cooperation among CSIRTs and regulatory bodies are welcomed.

**Incident Reporting Consolidation:**
Supporting consolidated incident reporting through single entry points rather than multiple parallel reporting requirements.

**Major Recommendation:**

**Eliminate SPOC Role:**
Remove the Single Point of Contact (SPOC) function, replacing it with clearer operational channels via:
- CSIRTs networks for technical coordination
- The cooperation group for policy coordination

This would streamline coordination mechanisms and eliminate redundant bureaucratic layers.

**Overall Assessment:**
The Recitals show positive direction in scope expansion and cooperation frameworks, but require revision to address threshold uniformity and avoid inappropriate DNS coverage that could undermine Internet architecture principles.
