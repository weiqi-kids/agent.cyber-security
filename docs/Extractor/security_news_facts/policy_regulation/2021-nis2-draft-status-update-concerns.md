## 摘要

CERT.at identifies significant issues in NIS2 draft requiring revision, including terminology ambiguity and unrealistic technical requirements.

## 事實內容

Otmar Lendl's November 2021 assessment of the NIS2 draft identifies fundamental issues requiring revision before year-end legislative completion.

**Key Concerns:**

**"National CSIRT" Terminology:**
The term lacks clear definition across multiple articles in the directive, creating ambiguity about which teams qualify as "national CSIRTs" versus other CSIRT types. This definitional gap affects mandate clarity and coordination responsibilities.

**Mandatory Threat Reporting Scope:**
Reporting triggers are too broad. Entities must report any "significant cyber threat," potentially overwhelming CSIRTs with routine risk assessments rather than actual incidents requiring coordination. The threshold for "significant" lacks clear definition.

**DNS Registration Requirements:**
DNS-related provisions appear disconnected from actual security goals, seemingly targeting cybercrime prevention rather than DNS infrastructure stability. These requirements may belong in e-commerce or law enforcement legislation rather than network security directives.

**Unrealistic Technical Capabilities:**
Parliamentary amendments impose unrealistic technical capabilities on CSIRTs and entities:
- Mandatory 24/7 staffing requirements
- Malicious traffic filtering capabilities
- Technical obligations that may not align with organizational capacity or threat models

**Procedural Concerns:**
High-speed text revisions have compromised consistency. Lendl advocates publishing a consolidated draft for fresh public comment rather than rushing implementation.

**Recommendations:**

**Terminology Clarification:**
Establish clear definitions distinguishing "national CSIRTs" from sectoral, organizational, and other CSIRT types.

**Reporting Threshold Refinement:**
Distinguish voluntary reporting from coordinated vulnerability disclosure. Clarify when incidents warrant CSIRT notification versus routine security operations.

**DNS Provisions Relocation:**
Move DNS registration requirements to e-commerce legislation if cybercrime prevention is the goal, rather than mixing law enforcement and security objectives.

**Scope Realignment:**
The analysis suggests fundamental scope misalignment rather than minor technical fixes. The directive attempts to address multiple policy goals (security, law enforcement, resilience) that may require separate legislative frameworks.

**Conclusion:**
CERT.at's assessment indicates the draft requires substantial revision rather than incremental refinement, with core definitional and scope issues undermining directive clarity.
