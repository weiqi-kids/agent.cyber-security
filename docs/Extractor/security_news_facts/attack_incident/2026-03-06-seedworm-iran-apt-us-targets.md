## Summary

Iranian APT group Seedworm (MuddyWater) found active on networks of U.S. bank, airport, and software company, deploying a new backdoor named Dindoor.

## Factual Content

The Iranian APT group Seedworm (also known as MuddyWater, Temp Zagros, Static Kitten) has been active on the networks of multiple U.S. companies since early February 2026. Activity has continued following U.S. and Israeli military strikes on Iran. Affected organizations include a U.S. bank, a software company (a supplier to defense and aerospace industries with a presence in Israel), an airport, and non-governmental organizations in both the U.S. and Canada.

A previously unknown backdoor named "Dindoor" was discovered on the networks of the Israeli outpost of the software company, with the same backdoor observed on the networks of a U.S. bank and a Canadian non-profit organization. Dindoor leverages Deno, the secure runtime for JavaScript and TypeScript, to execute. The backdoor was signed with a certificate issued to "Amy Cherne."

## Affected Systems

- U.S. banking infrastructure
- U.S. airport systems
- Defense/aerospace software company (Israel operations)
- Canadian NGO networks
- Windows systems (Dindoor backdoor via Deno runtime)
