## Summary

SecurityScorecard's STRIKE team disclosed "Operation WrtHug," a large-scale cyber campaign targeting ASUS routers globally, exploiting known vulnerabilities including CVE-2023-39780 to turn infected devices into global network threat tools, affecting over 50,000 devices.

## Factual Content

Security vendor SecurityScorecard's STRIKE team recently released an important report revealing a campaign codenamed "Operation WrtHug." This campaign is targeting ASUS-related routers globally, using known vulnerabilities to turn infected devices into tools for global network threat activities. TWCERT issued an emergency alert strongly recommending all ASUS router users immediately take necessary measures to ensure information security.

Operation WrtHug is a persistent attack campaign targeting small/home routers, currently observed to mainly concentrate on the ASUS brand. Attackers primarily target the AiCloud service on ASUS devices, exploiting a series of publicly disclosed OS Command Injection vulnerabilities (such as vulnerabilities related to CVE-2023-39780) as initial access points. Currently observed to affect 8 ASUS product models:

1. ASUS Wireless Router 4G-AC55U
2. ASUS Wireless Router 4G-AC860U
3. ASUS Wireless Router DSL-AC68U
4. ASUS Wireless Router GT-AC5300
5. ASUS Wireless Router GT-AX11000
6. ASUS Wireless Router RT-AC1200HP
7. ASUS Wireless Router RT-AC1300GPLUS
8. ASUS Wireless Router RT-AC1300UHP

Once routers are successfully compromised, hackers will plant backdoor programs, making devices join a massive global infection network to be used for launching further network threat activities and espionage operations. The STRIKE team has identified over 50,000 globally infected IP devices in the past six months.

Since attackers exploit publicly disclosed vulnerabilities with available patches, ensuring device security is crucial. All users are urged to immediately take the following three critical actions:

1. Executing firmware updates is critical: Be sure to update your router firmware to the latest version. This is the most direct method to patch known vulnerabilities and prevent intrusion.
2. Check and replace EoL devices: If you use "End-of-Life" (EoL) old ASUS routers that cannot update firmware, consider replacing these old devices with new products still officially supported.
3. Consult official resources: ASUS security team has provided official mitigation steps for all vulnerabilities involved in Operation WrtHug.
