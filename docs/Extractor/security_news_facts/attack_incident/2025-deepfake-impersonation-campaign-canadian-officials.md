## 摘要

Canadian authorities warn of AI-powered deepfake campaign impersonating senior government officials to defraud victims and steal sensitive information.

## 事實內容

The Canadian Anti-Fraud Centre, in coordination with cybersecurity officials, issued a joint advisory warning Canadians of a malicious campaign using AI-generated deepfakes to impersonate high-profile public figures.

**Attack Method:**

**Multi-Channel Impersonation:**
- **Text Messages (SMS)**: Fraudulent texts claiming to be from senior officials
- **AI-Generated Voice Calls**: Deepfake audio impersonating recognizable voices of government leaders and public figures
- **Spoofed Caller ID**: Display names and phone numbers falsified to appear legitimate

**Attack Objectives:**

**Information Theft:**
Deceiving victims into divulging sensitive information including:
- Personal identifying information
- Financial account details
- Corporate credentials
- Confidential communications

**Financial Fraud:**
Convincing victims to transfer funds based on urgent requests from purported senior officials.

**Primary Targets:**

**High-Value Individuals:**
- Business executives with financial authority
- Senior public officials with access to sensitive information
- Government employees with security clearances

**Broader Population:**
The campaign affects broader Canadian populations beyond initial high-value targets.

**Warning Signs:**

**Suspicious Communications:**
- Messages or calls from someone claiming to be a high-ranking government official (unusual for officials to make direct contact with private individuals)
- Urgent action requests demanding immediate response
- Suspicious links or attachments in messages
- Requests for personal or financial data

**Deepfake Indicators:**
- Unnatural voice tones or rhythms
- Mismatched audio (voice not synchronizing with claimed speaker's known patterns)
- Video artifacts or visual inconsistencies
- Unusual phrasing or language not typical of the purported speaker

**Defense Guidance:**

**Caller ID Skepticism:**
Don't trust caller IDs or display names—they can be easily spoofed using widely available tools.

**Independent Verification:**
Verify any unusual requests through known, official contact channels:
- Use publicly published phone numbers
- Contact organizations through official websites
- Confirm through multiple independent channels before taking action

**Link and Attachment Caution:**
Avoid clicking suspicious links or downloading attachments from unexpected messages, even if they appear to come from trusted sources.

**Financial Request Scrutiny:**
Scrutinize any urgent financial requests, particularly:
- Requests to transfer funds immediately
- Wire transfers to unusual accounts
- Cryptocurrency payments
- Changes to payment procedures

**Deepfake Detection:**
Train staff to identify audio and video inconsistencies:
- Unnatural facial movements or expressions
- Audio quality inconsistencies
- Lighting or shadow anomalies in video
- Background noise mismatches

**Incident Reporting:**

Report suspicious activity to:
- Local law enforcement agencies
- Canadian Anti-Fraud Centre (1-888-495-8501)
- Canadian Centre for Cyber Security (contact@cyber.gc.ca)

**Technological Context:**
The availability of AI-generated voice and video deepfake tools has lowered the barrier for sophisticated impersonation attacks, enabling criminals to create convincing fraudulent communications at scale.

**Implication:**
Traditional authentication methods relying on voice recognition or caller ID are insufficient against AI-powered impersonation. Organizations must implement multi-factor verification for sensitive requests.

## 受影響系統

- Business executives and senior officials
- Government employees
- Communication systems (SMS, voice calls)
- Caller ID systems (vulnerable to spoofing)
- Financial transaction authorization processes
