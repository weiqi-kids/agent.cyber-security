## 摘要
The CNCS Cybersecurity Observatory published the Risks & Conflicts 2021 Report, focusing on 2020 and projecting 2021-2022. Key findings include a significant increase in cybersecurity incidents and cybercrime, driven by COVID-19, with phishing, malware, ransomware, and fraud as leading threats.

## 事實內容
The Risks & Conflicts 2021 Report by the CNCS Cybersecurity Observatory was published. Key highlights:

**Main findings**:
- Significant increase in cybersecurity incidents and cybercrime indicators in 2020
- COVID-19 pandemic correlates with surges in incidents, coinciding with social confinement, remote work, and intensive digital use
- Most relevant cyber threats in 2020: phishing/smishing, malware-infected systems, ransomware, various types of intrusion, fraud/scams, sextortion, and digital disinformation
- Increased perception of risk of suffering a cybersecurity incident in 2020 and 2021
- Perception that Portuguese cyberspace is equally or better equipped in 2021 compared to 2020

**Report format**: Full document and "Report in 15 minutes" summary version available

**Data sources**: CNCS own data, contributions from partner institutions, public data from other entities, and community surveys.
